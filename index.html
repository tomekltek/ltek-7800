Browser loader · WebSerial

Send a game over USB.

  1. Cartridge in the 7800+, console on, USB-C to this computer (Chrome or Edge).
  2. Connect cartridge, pick the port called Pico.
  3. Choose a game, Load to cartridge. About a second for 32 KB.

The 7800+ reloads by itself when the bytes at $C000 change. On Ubuntu you may need: sudo usermod -aG dialout $USER, then log in again. Windows 11 needs nothing.

Game · saves through sound

Sokoban that saves through the TV speaker.

The 7800+ never writes anything back to the cartridge. I checked the bus with a sniffer. So the game plays its save as a few seconds of tones, a small sensor next to the TV hears them, and the cartridge keeps the save in flash. Switch the console off and on, and the game says LOADED FROM CART.

App · picture by sound

Send a picture to the Atari as sound.

The page turns a photo into 20 × 12 blocks in 7800 colours and plays it as tones. The sensor hears it, the cartridge stores it, and the console reloads with your picture on screen.

  1. Load LTEK Photo as the start-up game.
  2. Volume up, speaker 10–30 cm from the sensor, Send by sound. About 40 seconds.
Still a prototype: in my last test the first send got lost and the second one made it. Pictures don't touch your game saves.

Demo · origin and print run

Scan the cartridge, see where it came from.

For limited runs every cartridge can carry a signed certificate. It shows a QR code on the TV, your phone checks the signatures and shows the serial, like 1 of 25. The serial is not in the game, so the MD5 stays the same on every copy.

It proves origin and print run. It does not stop copying; the 7800+ reads the whole ROM anyway.
